# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Nick (owner) and Maria (admin) at DoorFlex LLC, a garage door repair company in Arizona. They use this dashboard daily to track parts inventory across warranty and subcontractor streams. Nick needs speed and clarity to verify deductions, spot low stock, and catch discrepancies without digging. Maria handles data entry, tech assignments, and part mapping. Both are non-technical and need the interface to be self-evident.

## Product Purpose

Track every part that leaves inventory against a signed paid invoice. Two business streams (warranty at 50% cost, subcontractor at 100% cost) under one roof, shared trucks, shared warehouse. The system deducts from the correct stream automatically so Nick never overpays for parts. No manual data entry from techs, just forwarded invoice PDFs.

## Brand Personality

Reliable, straightforward, professional. No fluff, no gimmicks. The interface should feel as solid as a well-installed garage door: it works, it's quiet, you don't think about it.

## Anti-references

- Overstyled SaaS dashboards with gradients, glassmorphism, and decorative charts
- Cluttered admin panels with too many cards and competing information densities
- Anything that looks like it belongs to a different industry (fintech, healthcare, crypto)

## Design Principles

1. **Information first.** Every screen answers one question. If a user has to hunt for the data, the layout failed.
2. **Low cognitive load.** Tables over cards. Clear sectioning. Search before scroll.
3. **Forgiving by design.** Delete actions confirm. Stock adjustments log automatically. Nothing is irreversible without a trace.
4. **Mobile-aware but desktop-native.** Used on a laptop in the office. Not a phone app. Layout prioritizes wide screens.
5. **Honest visual hierarchy.** Important things look important. Secondary actions look secondary. No false emphasis.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Basic WCAG AA contrast. Large enough click targets for someone who might be in a hurry. No critical interactions depend on color alone (stock status uses text labels in addition to color).
